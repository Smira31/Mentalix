# Competitor browser findings — 28 Aug 2026

## Clarity (App Store)
Source: https://apps.apple.com/us/app/clarity-cbt-self-help-journal/id1010391170
The listing shows 4.8 stars from 29K ratings, age 13+, Medical category. Its core promise is managing stress, anxiety and low mood through CBT-style thought reframing, cognitive-distortion work, mood/activity check-ins, guided journals, personalized daily plans, AI chatbot, assessments, educational programs, meditation audios, soundscapes, breathwork, tips and affirmations. Pro subscription unlocks Guided Journals, AI chatbots, assessments, programs, crash courses, audios, breathwork, tips, affirmations and advanced insights. It explicitly frames itself as self-help/education, not therapy or a medical device. Privacy claim: journal entries stored locally by default, optional backup.

## Daylio (Google Play)
Source: https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=net.daylio&hl=en_US
The listing shows 4.7 stars, 461K reviews, 10M+ downloads, in-app purchases. The differentiator is ultra-low-effort logging without typing: choose mood and activities, optionally add notes/photos/audio, then review charts/statistics to identify emotional patterns, behaviors and triggers. Positioning spans private diary, gratitude journal, mental-health support, anxiety/depression/bipolar tracking, therapy/CBT support, goals and habits.

## Product implications
Clarity monetizes structured psychological change tools and breadth, while Daylio monetizes low-friction tracking plus accumulated personal data and visual feedback. These are distinct jobs from Mentalix’s action-first “one next step”; they are potential reference patterns, not reasons to add every feature.

## Wysa (Google Play)
Source: https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=bot.touchkin&hl=en_US
The listing shows 4.7 stars, 152K reviews, 1M+ downloads and in-app purchases. Publicly visible positioning centers on an AI mental-wellbeing companion, mood journal, mindfulness coach, anxiety helper and mood-boosting buddy. The listing visibly emphasizes anonymity/non-judgment and includes sleep stories/sounds and relaxation exercises in the public review context. This suggests that users value immediate emotional support plus a concrete exercise, but the public listing alone does not prove clinical efficacy or exact premium conversion drivers.

## Finch (Google Play)
Source: https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.finch.finch&hl=en_US
The listing shows 4.9 stars, 615K reviews, 10M+ downloads and in-app purchases. Finch turns self-care into a lightweight daily loop: quick mood check-in, small exercises, habits, breathing, quizzes, mood trends, quotes and insights; completion grows a virtual pet and unlocks rewards. Morning check-in and evening gratitude are explicit parts of the loop. Its differentiator is emotional reward and low pressure, not depth of journaling.

## Reflectly (Google Play)
Source: https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.reflectlyApp&hl=en_US
The listing shows 4.5 stars, 40.7K reviews, 1M+ downloads and in-app purchases. Reflectly frames itself as an AI journal for venting thoughts, mood tracking, daily insights, personalized questions and affirmations. It combines positive psychology, mindfulness and CBT language, with daily/weekly/monthly overviews and mood correlations. Its paid-value hypothesis is personalized reflection and accumulated insight rather than a single isolated exercise.

## Headspace (Google Play)
Source: https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.getsomeheadspace.android&hl=en_US
The listing shows 4.6 stars, 340K reviews, 10M+ downloads and in-app purchases. It sells a large content library: 500+ guided meditations, sleep meditations, soundscapes, breathing, relaxation, focus, movement, licensed therapists/coaches and an AI companion. It is a content/subscription benchmark rather than a close product analogue for Mentalix.

## Calm (official site)
Source: https://www.calm.com/
Calm’s public value proposition is organized around three jobs: in-the-moment stress/anxiety relief, falling and staying asleep, and mindful resilience. The site leads with “Try Calm for Free” and reports over 2 million 5-star reviews as a vendor claim. Its value is packaged as a large, high-quality guided-content subscription, especially sleep and meditation; Mentalix should not imitate its content scale without a very different production and distribution model.
