# WTP and safety findings — 28 Aug 2026

## Willingness to pay
Liu, Xie and Or (2024) surveyed 577 adults about health-app willingness to pay. 58.9% said they were willing to pay; median stated WTP was HK$50 (approximately US$6.50 at the study's approximate conversion). The most common reasons for unwillingness were the belief that government should provide health apps for free, distrust of health apps, and lack of awareness of apps and their benefits. Prior installation and higher prior maximum payment were associated with greater WTP. Source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11064745/

A 2022 systematic review and meta-analysis of 30 articles representing 35 studies found that WTP for eHealth varied substantially. Monthly estimates ranged from US$5.25 to US$45.64 depending on population, modality and measurement method. WTP was higher for health apps and synchronous communication than for websites/asynchronous communication in the analyzed data, but the authors caution that estimates are method-dependent and heterogeneous. Source: https://www.jmir.org/2022/9/e25959/

## User value and risk
A 2022 umbrella review of 36 reviews found potential value in timely support, lower cost, reduced stigma and improved therapeutic outcomes. Recurrent pitfalls included engagement problems, emergency-safety issues, privacy/confidentiality breaches and non-evidence-based approaches. Source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9505389/

## Product implication
Payment should follow demonstrated repeated value, not precede it. The likely paid unit is not “more content” in the abstract, but a trusted, repeatable outcome: personalized pattern insight, structured problem-solving track, meaningful history/continuity, or higher-quality guided support. Privacy transparency and explicit self-help boundaries are prerequisites for trust, especially for AI and sensitive journaling.
